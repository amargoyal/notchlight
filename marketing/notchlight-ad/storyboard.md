## Reference study

The references informed the choices below; these are design interpretations, not measured advertising-performance claims.

- [Apple's Dynamic Island introduction](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2022/09/apple-debuts-iphone-14-pro-and-iphone-14-pro-max/) describes the interface expanding into different shapes while retaining its hardware relationship. The useful lesson here is object continuity: keep the notch anchored, and reveal features through its changing silhouette. [Launch-clip reupload](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-kX2o1Jz5tU) provides a visual reference for the product close-up; this is not an official Apple channel.
- [Ordinary Folk: Webflow — A New Era of No-Code](https://www.ordinaryfolk.co/project/webflow-a-new-era-of-no-code) provides inspectable styleframes and process imagery. Its focal hierarchy and object relationships informed the composition. The film credits a voice actor, so it is a motion-craft reference, not a silent-ad example.
- [Raycast's Hype Team](https://www.raycast.com/blog/hype-team) documents its dedicated mix of video, visual design and creative development. It is production-process context rather than evidence of a particular pacing formula.

The supplied 15-second Notchlight MP4 was also inspected via extracted frames. It had all the right ingredients but rushed the action/results, showed review-only sound cue labels, and attempted to click an item below the visible clipboard viewport. Those observations, rather than a generic target duration, motivated the extra time and interaction changes.

## Motion thesis

The hero is the camera cutout turning into useful space. The continuous desktop preserves location; distinct camera holds preserve comprehension. Cursor press compression, restrained click rings, the PDF landing and clipboard reorder explain cause and result. Text is sparse, and every major result has time to read without speech or audio. Sound effects are support, never the only way an action is communicated.
